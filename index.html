01 — Overview

Where Does Malaysia Grow Its Food?

Agriculture is woven into Malaysia's identity. From the rice bowl states of Kedah and Perlis in the northwest to the vast plantations of Sabah and Sarawak in East Malaysia, each state contributes differently to the national harvest.

This map shows total crop production (in metric tonnes) by state in 2022, the most recent complete year. Darker shades signal higher output. East Malaysia dominates due to the sheer scale of its industrial crops, while Peninsular states lead in food crops like paddy and vegetables.

02 — Crop Composition

Not One Crop, But Many

Malaysia grows an extraordinary variety of crops from industrial giants like palm oil and rubber to everyday staples like paddy, fruits, and vegetables. But the mix differs dramatically by state.

Sabah and Sarawak are almost entirely industrial crop territory. Meanwhile, states like Kedah are almost entirely paddy. Selangor and Johor show the most diverse profiles, growing everything from vegetables to fruits and cash crops.

This stacked bar chart shows the crop type composition for each state, letting you see which agricultural identity each state has claimed.

04 — Crop × State Patterns

Which State Grows What?

The heatmap below reveals the intensity of each crop type across every state. A quick scan shows which state-crop combinations dominate the national picture.

Efficiency: Area Planted vs. Output

Not all planted land produces equal returns. The scatter plot on the right maps each state's planted area against its total production states sitting above the trend line are punching above their weight in yield efficiency.

Kedah and Sabah represent opposite ends of the spectrum: Kedah produces consistently high paddy yields on modest land, while Sabah's massive planted area reflects extensive palm oil cultivation.

05 — Proportional Share

How Diverse Is Each State's Agriculture?

When we normalise production to 100%, the story shifts. States that look small on an absolute scale like Perlis or Putrajaya show surprisingly concentrated specialisations.

This view strips away the size effect and asks: what proportion of each state's harvest comes from which crop type? A state dominated by a single bar is highly specialised; a balanced mix of colours signals diversification.

07 — Forests as Farmland

Timber: East Malaysia's Industrial Forest

Sabah and Sarawak together account for the overwhelming majority of Malaysia's timber production. The multi-line chart on the left tracks log production from 2000 to 2021, showing a long-term decline as forest protection policies took hold a notable environmental success story.

Meanwhile, Peninsular Malaysia's timber sector has remained comparatively small and stable, reflecting both stronger enforcement and less remaining primary forest.

08 — The Bigger Picture

Agriculture's Shrinking Share of Malaysia's Economy

As Malaysia industrialised and urbanised, agriculture's contribution to GDP declined from over 30% in the 1970s to under 9% today. Yet the sector remains indispensable employing millions and feeding the nation.
